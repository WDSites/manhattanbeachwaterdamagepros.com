Manhattan Beach Water Damage Pros is a restoration service for homes in Manhattan Beach, California and the nearby towns of Hermosa Beach, Redondo Beach, El Segundo, Hawthorne and Lawndale. You call one number, (424) 550-5662, and the dispatcher routes your loss to licensed and insured restoration professionals.

We keep this page plain because the trade has a trust problem. Water damage companies often lean on claims nobody can check, so we explain below what we do, what we do not do, and what you should ask any company that comes to your door.

What happens when you call

A person answers the line 24/7. They ask where the water came from, how long it has been there, which rooms are wet and whether it looks clean or contaminated. Those answers decide whether the job starts with extraction, drying or containment.

Before any work begins, scope and price are explained. If the loss needs an inspection first, the dispatcher schedules it and tells you what the crew will measure. We do not promise an arrival time because traffic and crew availability change, though same-day response is available when crews are available.

What we do

We handle water damage from supply lines, appliances, roofs and drains, and we handle storm and sewage water under the flood service. We also handle mold that follows moisture. Work follows the stages on the services page, from extraction through repair.

Crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find hidden wet spots, then place air movers and dehumidifiers and check readings until each material reaches its drying goal. We keep photos and a drying log so your insurer has a record.

What we do not do

We do not decide coverage

Only your insurer decides what your policy covers. We document the loss and you take the record to your adjuster.

We are not a waterproofing trade

Restoration repairs water that already got in. If water keeps entering, ask a waterproofing or foundation contractor about that.

We do not diagnose health problems

Questions about how mold or water affects a person belong to a physician, not a restoration company.

We do not promise arrival times

Crews are dispatched as fast as availability and traffic allow, and the dispatcher tells you honestly what to expect.

Industry standards, named honestly

The published standards for the trade are ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration and S520 for mold remediation. They describe how drying goals, water categories and containment should work. We name them because they are real references, and we encourage you to ask any company whether its crews work to them.

We do not claim a firm certification or a warranty on this site, and we do not publish named technicians or reviews. What you get is a clear explanation by phone, a documented job and a crew that is licensed and insured.

How we explain scope and price

Restoration scope depends on facts nobody can see over the phone, such as how far moisture has traveled inside a wall. So we explain scope in plain terms first: what the crew will measure, what will probably be removed, what will be dried and what will be rebuilt.

Price follows scope. The factors that move it are the water category, the area affected, the materials involved, the equipment and days needed for drying, and how much rebuilding follows. We walk through those before work starts, so the number you hear is tied to something you can see.

Common questions

Who actually does the work?

Licensed and insured restoration professionals carry out the work, dispatched through this line. If you want details about the crew assigned to your job, ask the dispatcher on the call.

Do you publish prices?

No. Prices depend on the water category, the area and the materials, so they are explained after the dispatcher or crew has the details and before work starts.

How do I know a restoration company is legitimate?

Ask for proof of contractor licensing and insurance, ask whether the crew works to ANSI/IICRC S500 and S520, and get the scope explained before you sign anything. A company that avoids those questions is worth avoiding.

Do you work with insurance companies?

We document the damage so your insurer has what it needs, but only your insurer decides coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer about your specific loss.
