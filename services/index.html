Water damage is rarely one job. A burst pipe starts as extraction, becomes drying, turns into cleanup and ends as a rebuild, and each stage has its own tools and its own questions. These pages split the stages so you can read the one that matches where you are, and every one ends with a way to reach dispatch.

Most callers in Manhattan Beach begin with emergency water removal because water is still on the floor. Others call days later about a stained ceiling and land on water damage repair. The table below maps the situation you are in to the page that explains it.

How the services fit together on a real job

A typical loss runs through several of these pages in order. After a supply line fails under a sink, extraction removes the pooled water, mitigation dries the cabinets and subfloor, cleanup removes the swollen toe-kick and wet insulation, and repair puts the kitchen back together.

A flood or sewage loss adds containment at the start, and a leak that was missed for a week adds a mold inspection near the end. Because the stages overlap, one crew often carries a job from extraction through drying, which keeps the moisture records in one place for your insurer.

Emergency services versus planned work

Some pages are written for the first 48 hours. Extraction, mitigation, cleanup and flood restoration all carry emergency framing because every hour of standing water lets damage spread into framing and flooring. Repair and mold remediation are usually scheduled after the emergency has been stabilized, though a fast mold inspection is reasonable whenever you see growth.

If you are unsure which category you are in, call. The dispatcher asks where the water came from, how long it has been there and whether it looks clean, then explains which stage you are in and how scope and price will be explained before work starts.

Common questions

Do I have to call each service separately?

No. One call reaches the same 24/7 dispatch line, and the crew carries the job through the stages that apply. The pages are separate only so you can read about the stage you are in.

Which page should I read if I do not know what I have?

Start with the table above. If nothing matches, call and describe the water, the room and how long it has been there. The dispatcher will place you in the right stage.

Do you work on mold even if there was no leak?

Mold needs moisture, so the first question is always where the moisture comes from. Condensation, a slow plumbing leak or a roof leak can all feed it, and the mold remediation page explains how an inspection looks for the source.

Is every service available 24/7?

The dispatch line is answered 24/7 for emergencies. Planned repair and mold work is scheduled with you after the emergency stage has been stabilized.
